### II. Testing, Benchmarks, And Behavioural Evidence: violation (N1)
- Two tiers (FR-014). Benchmarks are acceptance criteria on a corpus instance declared in
  `project.yml` (`iDopaNeuroC_problem_LP`; SC-002, SC-003). The comparison is implemented once in
  the shared test module (FR-014, spec.md:374-375).
- Tolerances: the spec ties τ to the verification report's effective tolerances. Those are the
  solver's tolerances (`src/verify.rs:11-21`), as required. The 10⁻⁶ fallback (spec.md:102) has
  no stated grounding in a solver tolerance (see N5).
- **N1, a determinism premise contradicted by the spec's own evidence.**
  - The Clarifications say Gurobi's default LP method "may run concurrent algorithms, which the
    vendor documents as possibly non-deterministic" (spec.md:102-104). The Gurobi 13.0 `Method`
    entry confirms this: the default "will typically choose non-deterministic concurrent
    (`Method=3`) for an LP", which "can produce different optimal bases when run multiple times".
  - Yet SC-004 (spec.md:449-454) declares "N = 1 sequence (deterministic solves)". It requires
    the environment-reuse results to equal the one-shot results within the comparison tolerance.
  - SC-001 likewise requires every vector (x, y, z, …) of two independent Gurobi runs to agree
    within 10 τ. On a degenerate instance, a different optimal basis gives different vectors
    beyond any feasibility-based tolerance.
  - So, as written, a correct implementation can fail SC-001 and SC-004. The N = 1 justification
    rests on an assumption the spec itself refutes. The requirement is therefore not reliably
    testable (II; XV-A, "where the evidence supports one of two readings, say which and why";
    XV-B).
  - **Repair** (either option):
    - Fix the comparison and reuse runs to a deterministic configuration, passed through the
      options structure and stated in the Clarifications. For example, `method` set to a single
      algorithm with `threads` = 1, or Gurobi `Method` 4 or 5 through the raw passthrough. Then
      the "deterministic solves" claim holds by construction.
    - Or state that vector agreement is required only where the optimum is unique, and that
      otherwise the verified measures and the objective are compared.

  Also correct "deterministic solves" in SC-004 accordingly.
- Observation for the plan (carried from review 1): C-specific failing-input tests are needed for
  the invariants the C layer adds (null pointers, invalid sense characters, cone and option codes,
  a structure-size prefix smaller than the minimum).
- Observation: SC-005 says "deterministic outcome". The vendor says `SoftMemLimit` "termination
  may be non-deterministic for multi-threaded runs". The outcome (status "memory") is certain
  when the limit is far below need, but the plan should say so.

## Verdict: FAIL

Violations to repair before a new, fresh-context Gate 1 review:

1. **N1** (II, XV-A, XV-B; VI-B for CHK006 and CHK013): SC-004 declares "deterministic solves".
   Together with SC-001's vector agreement, it relies on deterministic Gurobi runs, but the spec
   and the vendor (`Method` default: non-deterministic concurrent, "different optimal bases") say
   otherwise. Either fix a deterministic configuration for the comparison and reuse runs, or
   restrict vector agreement to unique optima. Then re-verify CHK006 and CHK013.
2. **N2** (XII-C, Notation Convention): the comparison rule reuses `b`, the row right-hand side,
   for "the Rust API's value", and introduces `a` and `τ` outside the inventory (spec.md:97-99,
   435). State the rule in words, or amend the inventory through the maintainer.
3. **N3** (XII-A): bare keys at checklist:48 and 52, and at human-loop.md:16 and 28.
4. **N4** (Governance, Commits Carry In-Flight Manual Work): commit `710ae9a` omitted, and did not
   list, the in-flight governance changes. Record this, or squash with `2f62cce`.
5. **N5** (VI-A, II): decisions without rationale: the size-prefixed extension mechanism
   (FR-020), and the factor 10 and 10⁻⁶ fallback of the comparison rule.
6. **N6** (XV, record accuracy): human-loop.md:31-32 says constitution 3.0.0 is uncommitted in
   this worktree, but it is committed in `2f62cce`.
